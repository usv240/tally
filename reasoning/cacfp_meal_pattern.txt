CACFP MEAL PATTERN RULES FOR CHILD CARE
Source: 7 CFR 226.20, Requirements for meals, as in force on October 22, 2026, and USDA policy memorandum CACFP 06-2025 (December 20, 2024).
Scope: meals and snacks served to children in the Child and Adult Care Food Program (CACFP), including family child care homes. Rules for adult participants are out of scope unless stated.

DEFINITIONS

A child is a participant aged 1 year through 18 years.
An infant is a participant aged birth through 11 months.
The five meal components are: fluid milk, vegetables, fruits, grains, and meats/meat alternates.
A meal is one of: breakfast, lunch, supper, or snack.
A meal is reimbursable only if it contains every component required for that meal type for a child.
A food is creditable if it may count toward a meal component.
Whole grain-rich means the grain content of a product is between 50 and 100 percent whole grain with any remaining grains being enriched.
A breakfast cereal is a ready-to-eat, instant, or regular hot cereal as defined by the Food and Drug Administration in 21 CFR 170.3(n)(4).
Added sugars and total sugars are different quantities. Added sugars are the sugars added during processing, as listed on the Nutrition Facts label line "Includes X g Added Sugars". Total sugars include naturally occurring sugars such as lactose in milk and yogurt.

REQUIRED COMPONENTS BY MEAL TYPE (children ages 1 through 18)

Rule B1. A reimbursable breakfast for a child must contain all three of these components: fluid milk; vegetables or fruits or a portion of both; and grains.
Rule B2. At breakfast, a meat or meat alternate may be served in place of the entire grains component, but only up to 3 times per week. One ounce equivalent of meats/meat alternates credits equal to one ounce equivalent of grains.
Rule B3. If a meat or meat alternate replaces the grains component at breakfast for a fourth time in the same week, that breakfast does not meet the grains requirement.

Rule L1. A reimbursable lunch for a child must contain all five components: fluid milk, meats/meat alternates, vegetables, fruits, and grains.
Rule L2. A reimbursable supper for a child must contain all five components: fluid milk, meats/meat alternates, vegetables, fruits, and grains.
Rule L3. At lunch and at supper, a vegetable may be served to meet the entire fruit requirement. When two vegetables are served at lunch or supper in this way, the two vegetables must be two different kinds of vegetable.

Rule S1. A reimbursable snack for a child must contain two of the five components: fluid milk, meats/meat alternates, vegetables, fruits, grains.
Rule S2. Fluid milk and juice may not be the only two items in a reimbursable snack. Fruit juice, vegetable juice, and milk together may comprise only one component of the snack.
Rule S3. A snack of fluid milk and fruit juice is not reimbursable.
Rule S4. A snack of fluid milk and vegetable juice is not reimbursable.
Rule S5. A snack of one component only is not reimbursable.

FLUID MILK

Rule M1. Fluid milk must be served as a beverage, or on cereal, or a combination of both.
Rule M2. Children who are 1 year old must be served unflavored whole milk.
Rule M3. Children who are 2 through 5 years old must be served unflavored milk. The milk may be whole, reduced-fat (2 percent), low-fat (1 percent), or fat-free (skim).
Rule M4. Children who are 6 years old and older may be served whole, reduced-fat (2 percent), low-fat (1 percent), or fat-free (skim) milk, and the milk may be unflavored or flavored.
Rule M5. Flavored milk is not allowed for children aged 1 through 5 years.
Rule M6. Lactose-free and reduced-lactose milk that meet the fat content and flavor requirements for the child's age group may be served as fluid milk.
Rule M7. For children, yogurt does not count as fluid milk and may not be served in place of fluid milk. The yogurt-for-milk substitution exists only for adult participants.
Rule M8. A fluid milk substitute such as soy milk may be served for a non-disability reason only on a written request from the parent or guardian, and the substitute must be nutritionally equivalent to milk as set out in 7 CFR 226.20(g)(3).

VEGETABLES AND FRUITS

Rule V1. A vegetable serving may contain fresh, frozen, or canned vegetables, dry beans, peas, and lentils, or vegetable juice.
Rule V2. One cup of leafy greens counts as one half cup of vegetables.
Rule V3. Cooked dry beans, peas, and lentils may be counted as either a vegetable or as a meat alternate, but not as both in the same dish.
Rule F1. A fruit serving may contain fresh, frozen, canned, or dried fruits, or fruit juice.
Rule F2. One quarter cup of dried fruit counts as one half cup of fruit.
Rule J1. Juice must be pasteurized and full-strength to be creditable.
Rule J2. Fruit juice or vegetable juice may be served to meet the vegetable or fruit requirement at only one meal or snack per day. Juice served at a second meal or snack in the same day does not credit toward the vegetable or fruit component.

GRAINS

Rule G1. All grains must be made with enriched or whole grain meal or flour.
Rule G2. At least one grain serving per day, across all eating occasions, must be whole grain-rich.
Rule G3. A day on which no whole grain-rich grain is served to a child does not meet the whole grain-rich requirement.
Rule G4. Grain-based desserts do not count toward the grains requirement. Cookies, cakes, brownies, doughnuts, sweet rolls, toaster pastries, and granola bars are grain-based desserts and are not creditable as grains.
Rule G5. Breakfast cereals must contain no more than 6 grams of added sugars per dry ounce to be creditable.
Rule G6. A breakfast cereal that contains more than 6 grams of added sugars per dry ounce is not creditable as a grain.
Rule G7. The breakfast cereal sugar limit is measured in added sugars, not total sugars. The former limit of 6 grams of total sugars per dry ounce applied only through September 30, 2025 and no longer applies.

MEATS AND MEAT ALTERNATES

Rule P1. Creditable meats/meat alternates are: lean meat, poultry, or fish; alternate protein products; cheese; eggs; cooked dry beans, peas, and lentils; peanut butter and other nut or seed butters; nuts and seeds; yogurt; commercial tofu and soy products; or any combination of these.
Rule P2. Acorns, chestnuts, and coconuts do not credit as meat alternates.
Rule P3. Non-commercial and non-standardized tofu and soy products are not creditable.

YOGURT

Rule Y1. Yogurt is creditable as a meat alternate for children. Four ounces by weight or one half cup by volume of yogurt equals one ounce equivalent of meats/meat alternates.
Rule Y2. Yogurt may be plain or flavored, unsweetened or sweetened.
Rule Y3. Yogurt must contain no more than 12 grams of added sugars per 6 ounces, which is 2 grams of added sugars per ounce, to be creditable.
Rule Y4. A yogurt that contains more than 12 grams of added sugars per 6 ounces is not creditable as a meat alternate.
Rule Y5. The yogurt sugar limit is measured in added sugars, not total sugars. The former limit of 23 grams of total sugars per 6 ounces applied only through September 30, 2025 and no longer applies.
Rule Y6. A yogurt's total sugars figure alone does not determine whether it is creditable. The added sugars figure determines it.
Rule Y7. Frozen yogurt, drinkable yogurt products, homemade yogurt, yogurt flavored products, yogurt bars, yogurt covered fruits or nuts, and similar products are not creditable as yogurt.
Rule Y8. Soy yogurt is creditable as a meat alternate under the same added sugars limit as dairy yogurt.

FOOD PREPARATION AND SERVICE

Rule D1. Deep-fat fried foods that are prepared on-site cannot be part of a reimbursable meal. Deep-fat frying means cooking by submerging food in hot oil or other fat.
Rule D2. Foods that are pre-fried, flash-fried, or par-fried by a commercial manufacturer may be served, but must be reheated by a method other than frying.
Rule D3. Foods and beverages must not be used as punishments or rewards.
Rule D4. Under family style meal service, second meals may not be claimed for reimbursement.
Rule D5. A modified meal that does not meet the meal pattern is reimbursable only with a written medical statement signed by a State licensed healthcare professional or a registered dietitian.
Rule D6. A parent or guardian may supply one component of a reimbursable meal for a non-disability reason, provided the component meets the requirements and the provider supplies the remaining components.

INFANTS (birth through 11 months)

Rule I1. Infants follow a separate infant meal pattern. The child meal pattern does not apply to infants.
Rule I2. Breastmilk or iron-fortified infant formula must be served to infants at every meal and snack.
Rule I3. Fruit juice and vegetable juice must not be served to infants. An infant meal that includes juice is not reimbursable.
Rule I4. There is no whole grain-rich requirement for infants. The whole grain-rich requirement applies only to the child and adult meal patterns.
Rule I5. Yogurt served to infants must meet the same added sugars limit as yogurt served to children: no more than 12 grams of added sugars per 6 ounces.
Rule I6. Ready-to-eat breakfast cereals served to infants must meet the same added sugars limit as breakfast cereals served to children: no more than 6 grams of added sugars per dry ounce.
Rule I7. Cooked grains such as rice, quinoa, and pasta are not creditable in the infant meal pattern.
Rule I8. Cheese food and cheese spread are not creditable as a meat alternate for infants. Natural or processed cheese is creditable for infants.
Rule I9. Solid foods may be introduced around 6 months of age, or earlier or later when developmentally appropriate for the infant.
Rule I10. Deep-fat fried foods prepared on-site cannot be part of a reimbursable infant meal.
Rule I11. Infant cereal must be iron-fortified to be creditable.

WHAT THESE RULES DO NOT COVER

These rules do not set minimum portion sizes, state licensing ratios, reimbursement rates, or tiering. Questions about those topics cannot be decided from these rules.
